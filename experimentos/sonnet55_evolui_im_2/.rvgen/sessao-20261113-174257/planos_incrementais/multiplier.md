## Publica — incremento 1

Behavior: With operand_a and operand_b held fixed, changing only op changes which 32-bit slice of the 64-bit product is returned and which signedness is applied. MUL returns the low word, MULH the signed x signed high word, and MULHSU the signed x unsigned high word.
Coverage gap: Strategy item 1 (op selection). The only witness is the smoke test, which drives op=11 (MULHU) on the all-ones operand pair. No public-port evidence shows that op alone picks the low word (MUL) or applies signed interpretation (MULH, MULHSU).
Prerequisites: The smoke test passes: MULHU on the all-ones pair is correct, so the product path and the high-word slice are witnessed for op=11 and the harness reference is sound.; No other prerequisite is needed. The unit is combinational, so there is no state and no earlier-transaction dependency.
New factor: Selector value of op (the other three of its four values, with the operands unchanged)
Stimulus: Reuse the all-ones operand pair from the smoke test and leave both operands untouched. Drive op to each of the three remaining selector values (MUL, MULH, MULHSU) in turn, sampling result after it settles each time. Compare each full 32-bit result against the arbitrary-precision reference. The reference interprets the operands per op, multiplies exactly, reduces modulo 2^64, and slices the low or high word as the op requires. This pair gives four distinct expected values across the four ops, so a swapped or mis-decoded selector, or a wrong slice, shows up as a mismatch.
Support/witness path: None needed. The focal behavior is directly observable at the result port.
Public observations: result for op=00 on the all-ones pair equals the reference low word; result for op=01 on the all-ones pair equals the reference signed x signed high word; result for op=10 on the all-ones pair equals the reference signed x unsigned high word; The three results and the smoke-test MULHU result are pairwise distinct, which shows op selects the result
Forbidden: Reading internal signals or the hierarchy; Changing the operands between op values in this case; Adding operand-signedness isolation, operand-order, single-bit-walk, boundary-grid, random or statelessness checks in this case; Computing expected values by hand instead of from the reference; Driving non-0/1 bit values; Using any clock

## Publica — incremento 2

Behavior: With operand_b fixed as a small positive value (bit 31 = 0), flipping only operand_a[31] while its low bits stay fixed makes MULH and MULHSU treat operand_a as negative, so their high word becomes all ones. MULHU treats operand_a as a large positive value, so its high word stays a small positive number. For each high-word op, the result is the expected value from the arbitrary-precision reference.
Coverage gap: Strategy item 2: isolated signedness interpretation of operand_a. The suite has op routing on the all-ones pair only, where both operands have bit 31 set at once. It has no witness that operand_a[31] alone changes the high word for MULH and MULHSU (signed) versus MULHU (unsigned).
Prerequisites: Smoke test: MULHU is observable and correct on the all-ones pair.; Approved case 1: op alone selects among MUL, MULH, MULHSU and MULHU on a fixed operand pair, and the combinational result is observable after settling.
New factor: operand_a bit 31 (sign versus zero extension of operand_a) with operand_b held positive
Stimulus: Hold operand_b at a small positive constant with bit 31 clear. Use two operand_a values that differ only in bit 31: one positive, close to the largest positive signed value, and one with the same low 31 bits and bit 31 set. For each operand_a value, evaluate MULH, MULHSU and MULHU. Change only the operand_a bit 31 between the two groups, and change only op within a group. Compute every expected value with the independent reference: interpret per op, multiply exactly, reduce modulo 2^64, take the high 32 bits.
Support/witness path: None needed. The behavior is directly observable at the public result port, so no support sequence is required.
Public observations: result for MULH with operand_a[31]=0 and with operand_a[31]=1, each compared to the full 32-bit reference value; result for MULHSU with operand_a[31]=0 and with operand_a[31]=1, each compared to the full 32-bit reference value; result for MULHU with operand_a[31]=0 and with operand_a[31]=1, each compared to the full 32-bit reference value; With operand_a[31]=1, the MULH and MULHSU results are negative-extended (all ones) and differ from the MULHU result. With operand_a[31]=0, all three high-word results agree.
Forbidden: Varying operand_b[31] (that is strategy item 3); Swapping operand order (that is strategy item 4); Single-bit walks or boundary-grid sweeps (items 5 and 6); Random regression (item 7); Re-testing the all-ones pair, which is already witnessed; Re-checking op routing as the focal behavior; Accessing internal signals; Driving metavalues; Using a clock; Expected values taken from the component

## Publica — incremento 3

Behavior: With operand_a fixed as a small positive value, operand_b is read as signed by MULH and as unsigned by MULHSU and MULHU. Setting operand_b[31] therefore makes the MULH high word negative, while MULHSU and MULHU stay equal and positive.
Coverage gap: Strategy item 3: signedness extension of operand_b in isolation. No passing test flips operand_b[31] alone. MULH (signed b) and MULHSU/MULHU (unsigned b) have not been separated on the operand_b side. Items 4 through 8 are also uncovered, but they depend on this behavior.
Prerequisites: Op selection across all four op values on a fixed operand pair (lote-001-caso-01 and the smoke test); operand_a sign-extension isolated for MULH, MULHSU and MULHU (lote-002-caso-01); The independent arbitrary-precision reference with mod-2^64 reduction is already used in the passing suite
New factor: Flipping only operand_b[31] with operand_a held small positive
Stimulus: Hold operand_a as a small positive value with bit 31 clear. Use two operand_b values that differ only in bit 31 and share the same low bits, one with bit 31 clear and one with it set. For each operand_b value, run the three high-word ops (MULH, MULHSU, MULHU) and compare each full 32-bit result to the reference. Then check three relations. First, with operand_b[31]=0, all three high words agree. Second, with operand_b[31]=1, MULHSU equals MULHU. Third, with operand_b[31]=1, MULH differs from both.
Support/witness path: None needed. The behavior is directly observable at the public result port, because the multiplier is purely combinational.
Public observations: result is compared in full (32 bits) against the reference for every (operand_b value, op) combination; With operand_b[31]=0, the MULH, MULHSU and MULHU results are equal; With operand_b[31]=1, MULHSU equals MULHU, and MULH is the all-ones negative-product word, which differs from both
Forbidden: Do not flip operand_a[31]; that relation is already witnessed; Do not use a negative operand_a, which would be the separate MULHSU/MULHU split on the operand_a side; Do not sweep operand values or add random vectors; Do not add operand-order swap, single-bit walk, boundary grid or hidden-state checks in this case; Do not observe or rely on internal signals; Do not hand-write binary or instruction encodings

## Publica — incremento 4

Behavior: For a full-width product whose low 32 bits and high 32 bits are both nonzero and different, MUL returns exactly P64[31:0] and each of MULH, MULHSU and MULHU returns exactly P64[63:32]. The expected value for each op comes from the independent arbitrary-precision reference reduced modulo 2^64.
Coverage gap: Strategy item 5 (result slicing): no case yet shows that the low-word slice and the high-word slice are taken from the correct bit positions of P64 when both words are nonzero and different. Existing cases use only all-ones, a small operand (7), and sign-bit flips. Their products have a trivial low or high word, so a swapped or shifted slice could still pass.
Prerequisites: Smoke and op-selection witnesses: op alone selects among the four results on the all-ones pair; Operand_a sign-extension witness for MULH, MULHSU and MULHU; Operand_b sign-extension witness for MULH, MULHSU and MULHU
New factor: Bit position of the slice boundary in P64 for a product with distinct, nonzero low and high words
Stimulus: Hold one fixed operand pair with mixed bit patterns and bit 31 set in both operands, as in strategy item 5 (for example 0x12345678 x 0x9ABCDEF1). Sweep all four op values over that pair with the operands unchanged. Compare each full 32-bit result against the reference: MUL against the low word, and the three high-word ops against the high word under their own signedness. Also check that the MUL result differs from the MULHU result for this pair, so the low word is not confused with the high word.
Support/witness path: None needed. The result is directly observable on the public result port, and the sweep is a pure combinational drive-and-read.
Public observations: result for op=0x0 equals reference P64[31:0] for the pair; result for op=0x1 equals reference signed x signed P64[63:32]; result for op=0x2 equals reference signed x unsigned P64[63:32]; result for op=0x3 equals reference unsigned x unsigned P64[63:32]; MUL result differs from MULHU result for the pair
Forbidden: Do not use single-bit walks or boundary-value grids, which belong to other strategy items.; Do not repeat the sign-extension isolation or operand-order experiments.; Do not use the randomized regression, which would be an enumeration.; Do not add the combinational-history check, which needs prior state.; Do not reference internal signals or the CPU parent.; Do not drive non-0/1 bits.
