# Component semantic contract: cpu

## Public interface
```vhdl
entity cpu is
    port(
        clk : in std_logic;
        rst : in std_logic;
        instr_addr : out std_logic_vector(31 downto 0);
        instr_data : in std_logic_vector(31 downto 0);
        data_addr : out std_logic_vector(31 downto 0);
        data_write : out std_logic;
        data_width : out std_logic_vector(1 downto 0);
        data_write_data : out std_logic_vector(31 downto 0);
        data_read_data : in std_logic_vector(31 downto 0)
    );
end entity cpu;
```

## Responsibility
Implement the RV32IM single-cycle top level: maintain the PC, instantiate the immediate generator, register file, ALU, decoder, and multiply/divide unit, select branch and jump targets, perform load extraction and extension, and drive the instruction and data buses exactly as specified.

## Timing

- `cpu` is an RV32IM single-cycle processor. `instr_addr` exposes the byte address of the current instruction; `instr_data` is the asynchronous instruction word at that address.
- With `rst = '0'`, each rising edge of `clk` completes the instruction currently presented at `instr_addr`. Register writes and external stores take effect on that edge, and the PC takes its next value.
- Decode, register reads, effective-address generation, bus outputs, load extraction, writeback selection, branch decisions, and next-PC selection are combinational. They respond to current inputs without waiting for a clock edge.
- `data_read_data` is the asynchronous little-endian word based at `data_addr & 0xFFFFFFFC`. A load uses its value when the load completes at the rising edge.
- The fixed decomposition is mandatory: `cpu` instantiates exactly one each of `immediate_generator`, `register_file`, `alu`, `decoder`, and `muldiv`. The CPU must not reimplement any of their behavior. The immediate generator, ALU, decoder, and muldiv are combinational; the register file provides the state and edge behavior specified by its own contract.
- The CPU connects `rs1_data`, `rs2_data`, and `instr[14:12]` to the single `muldiv` instance. It selects the muldiv result for writeback when the decoder asserts `m_enable`. The M result must be ready in the same cycle as every other instruction result.

## Reset

- `rst` is active high and asynchronous.
- While `rst = '1'`, `instr_addr` is `0x00000000`, and architectural registers read as zero. Reset does not require a clock edge.
- A rising edge while reset is asserted does not execute an instruction, update the PC, write a register, or cause an external data-memory write. `data_write` is `'0'` throughout reset.
- Deasserting reset does not itself advance the PC or change register contents. Execution resumes from address zero at the next rising edge.

## Behavior

- The public interface remains the exact `cpu` entity and ports specified for this component. The CPU must not add public ports, generics, or external memory instances.
- The PC is a 32-bit byte address. For each executed instruction, its next value is selected in this order:
  1. `JAL` or valid `JALR`: use the corresponding jump target.
  2. A taken conditional branch: use the branch target.
  3. Otherwise: use `PC + 4`.
  
  PC arithmetic wraps modulo \(2^{32}\). `JAL x0, 0` leaves the PC unchanged on each rising edge.
- `JAL` writes `PC + 4` to `rd` and targets `PC + imm_j`. Valid `JALR` writes `PC + 4` to `rd` and targets `(rs1 + imm_i) & 0xFFFFFFFE`. A branch target is `PC + imm_b`.
- The register file has 32 architectural 32-bit registers. `x0` always reads as zero, and writes to `x0` have no effect. Register reads are asynchronous; a valid write takes effect on the rising edge completing the instruction.
- `data_addr` is the effective byte address for loads and stores: `rs1 + imm_i` for loads and `rs1 + imm_s` for stores. Its low bits are not masked.
- For loads and stores, `data_width` is `"00"` for byte, `"01"` for halfword, and `"10"` for word. `"11"` is not selected by a valid load or store.
- For loads, `data_read_data` is interpreted as a little-endian word based at `data_addr & 0xFFFFFFFC`. The address low bits select the byte or halfword. `LB` and `LH` sign-extend; `LBU` and `LHU` zero-extend; `LW` uses the full word.
- For valid stores, `data_write` is `'1'` during the store instruction cycle, `data_write_data` is the unshifted `rs2` value, and `data_width` identifies which low 8, 16, or 32 bits are written. The external memory commits the write on the rising edge. For every non-store instruction, and during reset, `data_write` is `'0'`.
- `data_addr`, `data_width`, and `data_write_data` have no architectural meaning when `data_write = '0'` and the current instruction is not a load. For loads, `data_addr` and `data_width` identify the effective address and access size.
- Unsupported opcodes and unsupported function encodings have no architectural side effects: they do not write a register or memory, do not branch or jump, do not enable the M-result path, and advance the PC by four. Their data-bus outputs other than `data_write = '0'` have no architectural meaning.
- The immediate generator supplies all five immediates concurrently, regardless of opcode:
  - `imm_i = sext(instr[31:20])`
  - `imm_s = sext(instr[31:25] || instr[11:7])`
  - `imm_b = sext(instr[31] || instr[7] || instr[30:25] || instr[11:8] || 0)`
  - `imm_u = instr[31:12] || 12'b0`
  - `imm_j = sext(instr[31] || instr[19:12] || instr[20] || instr[30:21] || 0)`

  Here `sext` is sign extension to 32 bits. The CPU selects the applicable immediate by instruction class: `imm_i` for OP-IMM and loads, `imm_s` for stores, and `imm_u` for AUIPC. LUI writes `imm_u` directly. Branch and jump targets use `imm_b` and `imm_j`, respectively.
- The decoder’s `alu_src_a_pc` selects PC as operand A when asserted, and `rs1` otherwise. Its `alu_src_b_imm` selects an applicable immediate as operand B when asserted, and `rs2` otherwise. These controls do not select the immediate format; the CPU supplies the applicable immediate.
- The decoder’s `wb_select` selects ALU result (`"00"`), data-memory read result (`"01"`), or `PC + 4` (`"10"`). For `"00"`, the CPU selects the muldiv result instead of the ALU result when `m_enable` is asserted. `"11"` is reserved and is not selected by a valid instruction.
- For the existing RV32I ALU path, the CPU adapts the decoder’s non-branch `alu_control` to the ALU’s selector as follows:

  | Decoder selector | Meaning | ALU selector |
  |---|---|---|
  | `"0000"` | ADD | `"0000"` |
  | `"0001"` | SUB | `"0001"` |
  | `"0010"` | SLL | `"0101"` |
  | `"0011"` | signed SLT | `"1000"` |
  | `"0100"` | unsigned SLTU | `"1001"` |
  | `"0101"` | XOR | `"0100"` |
  | `"0110"` | SRL | `"0110"` |
  | `"0111"` | SRA | `"0111"` |
  | `"1000"` | OR | `"0011"` |
  | `"1001"` | AND | `"0010"` |
  | `"1010"` | PASS_B for LUI | Do not rely on an ALU PASS_B operation; select `imm_u` directly for LUI writeback |

  The decoder’s branch selector is not sent to the ALU as a non-branch operation selector. The CPU uses the ALU’s `equal`, `signed_less`, and `unsigned_less` outputs to evaluate the selected branch condition. Those comparison outputs are defined independently of the ALU operation selector.

## Operations

Instruction fields are `opcode = instr[6:0]`, `rd = instr[11:7]`, `funct3 = instr[14:12]`, `rs1 = instr[19:15]`, `rs2 = instr[24:20]`, and `funct7 = instr[31:25]`. The decoder recognizes the following encodings; all unlisted function encodings are unsupported.

| Instruction | Opcode | Valid function encoding | Architectural operation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `LUI` | `0x37` | Any `funct3`, `funct7` | `rd = U` |
| `AUIPC` | `0x17` | Any `funct3`, `funct7` | `rd = PC + U` |
| `JAL` | `0x6F` | Any `funct3`, `funct7` | `rd = PC + 4`; jump to `PC + J` |
| `JALR` | `0x67` | `funct3 = 000` | `rd = PC + 4`; jump to `(rs1 + I) & 0xFFFFFFFE` |
| `BEQ` | `0x63` | `funct3 = 000` | Branch if `rs1 == rs2` |
| `BNE` | `0x63` | `funct3 = 001` | Branch if `rs1 != rs2` |
| `BLT` | `0x63` | `funct3 = 100` | Branch if signed `rs1 < rs2` |
| `BGE` | `0x63` | `funct3 = 101` | Branch if signed `rs1 >= rs2` |
| `BLTU` | `0x63` | `funct3 = 110` | Branch if unsigned `rs1 < rs2` |
| `BGEU` | `0x63` | `funct3 = 111` | Branch if unsigned `rs1 >= rs2` |
| `LB` | `0x03` | `funct3 = 000` | Load signed byte |
| `LH` | `0x03` | `funct3 = 001` | Load signed halfword |
| `LW` | `0x03` | `funct3 = 010` | Load word |
| `LBU` | `0x03` | `funct3 = 100` | Load unsigned byte |
| `LHU` | `0x03` | `funct3 = 101` | Load unsigned halfword |
| `SB` | `0x23` | `funct3 = 000` | Store low byte of `rs2` |
| `SH` | `0x23` | `funct3 = 001` | Store low halfword of `rs2` |
| `SW` | `0x23` | `funct3 = 010` | Store `rs2` word |
| `ADD` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 000`, `funct7 = 0000000` | `rd = rs1 + rs2` |
| `SUB` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 000`, `funct7 = 0100000` | `rd = rs1 - rs2` |
| `SLL` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 001`, `funct7 = 0000000` | `rd = rs1 << rs2[4:0]` |
| `SLT` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 010`, `funct7 = 0000000` | `rd = 1` iff signed `rs1 < rs2` |
| `SLTU` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 011`, `funct7 = 0000000` | `rd = 1` iff unsigned `rs1 < rs2` |
| `XOR` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 100`, `funct7 = 0000000` | `rd = rs1 XOR rs2` |
| `SRL` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 101`, `funct7 = 0000000` | Logical right shift by `rs2[4:0]` |
| `SRA` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 101`, `funct7 = 0100000` | Arithmetic right shift by `rs2[4:0]` |
| `OR` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 110`, `funct7 = 0000000` | `rd = rs1 OR rs2` |
| `AND` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 111`, `funct7 = 0000000` | `rd = rs1 AND rs2` |
| `MUL` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 000`, `funct7 = 0000001` | Low 32 bits of `rs1 × rs2` |
| `MULH` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 001`, `funct7 = 0000001` | High 32 bits of signed × signed product |
| `MULHSU` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 010`, `funct7 = 0000001` | High 32 bits of signed `rs1` × unsigned `rs2` product |
| `MULHU` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 011`, `funct7 = 0000001` | High 32 bits of unsigned × unsigned product |
| `DIV` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 100`, `funct7 = 0000001` | Signed quotient, truncated toward zero |
| `DIVU` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 101`, `funct7 = 0000001` | Unsigned quotient |
| `REM` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 110`, `funct7 = 0000001` | Signed remainder; nonzero remainder has the sign of `rs1` |
| `REMU` | `0x33` | `funct3 = 111`, `funct7 = 0000001` | Unsigned remainder |
| `ADDI` | `0x13` | `funct3 = 000` | `rd = rs1 + I` |
| `SLLI` | `0x13` | `funct3 = 001`, `instr[31:25] = 0000000` | `rd = rs1 << instr[24:20]` |
| `SLTI` | `0x13` | `funct3 = 010` | `rd = 1` iff signed `rs1 < I` |
| `SLTIU` | `0x13` | `funct3 = 011` | `rd = 1` iff unsigned `rs1 < I` |
| `XORI` | `0x13` | `funct3 = 100` | `rd = rs1 XOR I` |
| `SRLI` | `0x13` | `funct3 = 101`, `instr[31:25] = 0000000` | Logical right shift by `instr[24:20]` |
| `SRAI` | `0x13` | `funct3 = 101`, `instr[31:25] = 0100000` | Arithmetic right shift by `instr[24:20]` |
| `ORI` | `0x13` | `funct3 = 110` | `rd = rs1 OR I` |
| `ANDI` | `0x13` | `funct3 = 111` | `rd = rs1 AND I` |

For `MUL`, only product bits `[31:0]` are returned. `MULH`, `MULHSU`, and `MULHU` return product bits `[63:32]`. `MULHSU` treats `rs1` as signed and `rs2` as unsigned. M instructions use register operands and select their result through the M-result writeback path when `m_enable` is asserted.

## Edge cases

- All architectural integer results, effective addresses, PC additions, and PC-relative targets wrap modulo \(2^{32}\); arithmetic overflow raises no exception.
- Shift amounts use only the low five bits of the relevant source. `SRA` and `SRAI` replicate the source sign bit; logical shifts fill vacated bits with zero.
- `SLT`, `SLTU`, `SLTI`, and `SLTIU` produce exactly zero or one.
- `JALR` clears target bit zero after adding its immediate. `JAL` and branch immediates have an implicit zero at bit zero.
- `JAL x0, 0` is a valid self-loop and keeps `instr_addr` unchanged on successive rising edges.
- Signed division and remainder truncate toward zero. For example, `-7 / 2 = -3` and `-7 rem 2 = -1`.
- For `DIV` and `DIVU` with divisor zero, the result is `0xFFFFFFFF`. For `REM` and `REMU` with divisor zero, the result is the dividend (`rs1`).
- For signed overflow, `DIV 0x80000000 / 0xFFFFFFFF` returns `0x80000000`, and `REM 0x80000000 rem 0xFFFFFFFF` returns `0x00000000`. These are defined results, not traps or exceptions.
- M instructions produce no exception, trap, or extra state. Writes to `x0` remain ignored.
- The external data-memory interface assumes naturally aligned accesses. Behavior for misaligned halfword or word accesses is unspecified.
- Inputs are defined for binary `std_logic` values. Behavior for unknown or high-impedance values is unspecified.
- No instruction can simultaneously be a jump and a conditional branch: their opcodes are distinct. The specified next-PC ordering therefore has no reachable overlap case.

## Test strategy

Use executable Cocotb tests with a software reference model for the PC, architectural register values, instruction behavior, and external memory. Observe only the CPU’s public ports; do not require direct reads of internal registers or selectors. Drive inputs, allow combinational propagation to settle, check bus outputs before the active edge, then apply the rising edge and check `instr_addr` and externally observable effects. Use binary inputs and distinct values so incorrect sources, slices, or selections are observable.

Observe register effects through later public behavior, such as storing a result to external memory or using it in a branch. Check `x0` by using it as a later store source or branch operand.

1. **Fetch, sequencing, reset, and edge qualification**
   - Assert reset asynchronously and require `instr_addr = 0`; deassert reset without a clock and require it remains zero.
   - Execute sequential instructions and check that `instr_addr` is the current PC before each edge and the expected next PC after it. Hold inputs constant while the clock is low and verify the PC does not advance.
   - Assert reset between edges after changing PC and registers. Require the PC to become zero immediately, `data_write = '0'`, and registers to read as zero through subsequent public behavior.
   - Hold reset across a rising edge with inputs that would otherwise cause writes; verify no register or memory side effect. Deassert reset without a clock and verify state does not advance until the next rising edge.

2. **Register effects and writeback routing**
   - Initialize registers through executed instructions using distinct, nonzero values. Verify results through later stores or branches, without inspecting internal state.
   - Exercise writeback from OP, OP-IMM, M instructions, LUI, AUIPC, loads, JAL, and JALR. Use each result in a later public operation.
   - Repeat representative writes with `rd = x0` and verify subsequent reads still observe zero. Reset after populating registers and verify reset-cleared values through public behavior.

3. **RV32I operations and ALU routing**
   - Execute every valid OP and OP-IMM encoding in **Operations**, checking results through later stores or branches. Use asymmetric operands such as `0x81234567` and `0x10203005`.
   - Test ADD/ADDI and SUB wraparound and signed boundaries, including `0xFFFFFFFF + 1`, `0x7FFFFFFF + 1`, and `0 - 1`.
   - For SLL/SLLI/SRL/SRLI/SRA/SRAI, test amounts 0, 1, and 31, plus sources with different upper bits but matching low five bits. Use negative and nonnegative operands to distinguish shift fill.
   - For signed and unsigned comparisons, test equal operands and pairs such as `0xFFFFFFFF` and `0x00000000`; verify results are exactly zero or one.
   - For XOR, OR, and AND, use mixed bit patterns and verify exact results.

4. **RV32M operations and writeback**
   - Execute all eight M encodings with `funct7 = 0000001`, using distinct operands and checking results through later stores or branches. Verify that results use `rs1`, `rs2`, and the instruction’s `funct3`.
   - Test product slices and signedness with operands including `(0xFFFFFFFF, 0x80000001)`, `(0x80000000, 0x80000000)`, and `(0xFFFFFFFF, 0xFFFFFFFF)`. Check low product bits for `MUL` and high bits for each high-product instruction.
   - Check signed division and remainder with positive and negative operands, including `-7 / 2 = -3`, `-7 rem 2 = -1`, and negative divisors. Check unsigned division and remainder with values whose signed interpretations differ.
   - Test division by zero for all four division/remainder operations: quotient results must be `0xFFFFFFFF`, and remainder results must equal the current dividend.
   - Test signed overflow with `0x80000000` and `0xFFFFFFFF`: `DIV` must return `0x80000000` and `REM` must return zero.
   - Verify M operations complete in one cycle, write the selected result on the completing edge, and have no memory, branch, or jump side effects. Test `rd = x0` to verify the result is discarded.

5. **Instruction validity and unsupported encodings**
   - Exercise unsupported OP function encodings, including `funct7` values other than the valid base or M encodings, and unsupported OP-IMM, load, store, branch, and JALR function encodings.
   - For each unsupported instruction, verify no register or memory write, no branch or jump redirect, no M-result effect, and sequential PC advance. Include representative unsupported opcodes.

6. **Immediate selection and transformations**
   - Construct I-, S-, B-, U-, and J-format instructions with distinguishable nonzero immediate slices. Verify the effective address, result, or target uses the correct format and bit ordering.
   - Test positive and negative I, S, B, and J immediates, including sign-extension effects. For U-type results, verify the low 12 bits are zero and the upper 20 bits match the instruction.
   - Test branch and jump offsets with nonzero encoded bits to verify the implicit low zero bit and target calculation.

7. **Branches and control flow**
   - Test BEQ and BNE with equal and unequal operands. Test BLT, BGE, BLTU, and BGEU with both outcomes, including `0xFFFFFFFF` versus `0x00000000` to distinguish signed from unsigned comparisons.
   - For each branch condition, check taken and not-taken next PCs, forward and backward targets, and that a not-taken branch uses `PC + 4`.
   - Test JAL and valid JALR redirects separately. For JAL, check positive and negative offsets and verify `PC + 4` link values through later public behavior. For JALR, check positive and negative I immediates, bit-zero clearing, and the link value.
   - Check invalid JALR function encodings do not write a link or redirect. Execute `JAL x0, 0` repeatedly and require `instr_addr` to remain unchanged on every edge.

8. **Load address, width, extraction, and extension**
   - For LB, LBU, LH, LHU, and LW, use a base and immediate that produce a distinguishable effective byte address. Check that `data_addr` is unmasked and `data_width` is correct.
   - Use a word with four distinct bytes, such as `0xD3A57C19`, to test all byte offsets, including bytes with the high bit set and clear. Verify byte selection and sign or zero extension through later public behavior.
   - Test halfword offsets 0 and 2 with distinct lower and upper halfwords, including signed-positive and signed-negative values. Verify LH sign extension and LHU zero extension; verify LW preserves all 32 bits.
   - Change only `data_read_data` between otherwise identical load executions and verify the loaded value follows the current input. Require loads never to assert `data_write`.

9. **Store routing and suppression**
   - For SB, SH, and SW, use distinct base, immediate, and `rs2` patterns. Check the unmasked effective byte address, `data_write = '1'`, correct width, and unshifted `rs2` payload.
   - Verify external memory commits only the low 8, 16, or 32 bits as appropriate and only on the rising edge. Change `rs2` before the edge and require the bus payload to track it; change it after the edge without another edge and verify no second store occurs.
   - For every non-store class, including unsupported instructions, require `data_write = '0'`. While reset is asserted, require `data_write = '0'` even if `instr_data` encodes a store.
